# The impact of body-positive and fitspirational influencers on body satisfaction: a longitudinal study of evolving parasocial relationships
*Priska L. Breves, Sophie C. Boerman,Jan-Philipp Stein,Carolin Ischen,Zeph M.C. van Berlo  (2025)*


---

## Article Reflection

 This study empirically demonstrates that the qualitative characteristics of content are a more decisive factor in forming Parasocial Relationships with influencers than simple exposure frequency. In other words, the 'nature of the content' has a deeper impact on user psychology than the 'quantity of exposure'. Following the nationwide fitness increase since the pandemic, numerous fitness influencers have emerged on social media, but according to the research, if they exclusively show perfectly and idealized bodies, there are limits to forming emotional bonds with users regardless of exposure frequency. This suggests that 'downstream effects' which lead to actual attitudinal changes or self-acceptance do not occur in such cases. For an influencer to exert genuine social influence, the formation of a parasocial relationship must come first. To achieve this, 'Authenticity,' characterized by sharing honesty and human vulnerabilities rather than the display of perfection, serves as the core psychological mechanism. Recently, I have seen an analysis that the factor of success of an idol lies in 'Authenticity'. This fact suggests that the findings of this study well explain the relationship between influencer and the public.

---

## Discussion

### 1
If participants had directly chosen influencers matching their own preferences, would the outcomes for PSR, Wishful Identification, and social comparison have differed? This study excluded the core social media characteristic of 'Selective Exposure' by forcing participants to follow a researcher-assigned influencer (Lively Izzy). As noted in the paper, individuals tend to select and emulate media figures they already perceive as likable or successful. therefore, questions arise as to whether data measured in a 'forced relationship' can fully represent the psychological dynamics of an actual 'voluntary relationship.


---

### 2
Do the same psychological mechanisms apply to virtual (VR/Virtual) influencers? This study found that authenticity is secured and parasocial relationships are strengthened when actual human influencers share vulnerabilities, such as revealing their own 'body imperfections.' In the case of highly popular virtual influencers or VR idols, would these findings apply identically? For instance, since virtual influencers' bodies often have physically impossible proportions, even if social comparison occurs, it is likely to remain a detached 'fantasy' rather than leading to actual changes in 'body satisfaction.' This suggests that the study's H3 results might manifest differently for VR influencers; thus, a discussion is needed on whether the findings regarding parasocial relationships, social comparison, and wishful identification would apply identically or show differences despite the absence of an 'actual body.'

---

### 3
What measurement methods or research designs could be considered to reduce the risk of 'Social Desirability Bias' in this study? Since this study handles sensitive topics like body image through self-reported surveys, there is a concern that respondents might have answered more positively than they actually feel. I believe that if 'parasocial relationships' had been measured through actual SNS behaviors—such as liking, commenting, saving, or sharing posts—rather than simple surveys, it could have served as an even more robust indicator.

---

## Summary

- This study shows that content quality and perceived authenticity are more important than simple exposure frequency in building parasocial relationships with influencers.
- Body-positive communication that includes human vulnerability strengthens emotional bonds, while idealized fitspiration content alone has limits in improving body satisfaction.
- The findings raise further questions about voluntary versus forced influencer selection, applicability to virtual influencers, and more behavior-based measures beyond self-report surveys.
