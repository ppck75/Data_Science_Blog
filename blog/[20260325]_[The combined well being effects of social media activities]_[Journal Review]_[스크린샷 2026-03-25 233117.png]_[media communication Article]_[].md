## Article Reflection

Numerous studies have already documented the negative impact of upward social comparison on self-esteem within social media environments. This study distinguishes itself by demonstrating that the act of recording and reflecting on one?셲 own positive narratives can serve as a substantive "psychological shield" amidst the cycle of relative deprivation. I found the insight particularly compelling: by linking Self-Affirmation Theory with Instagram?셲 inherent curation characteristics, the research shows that the act of selective recording itself generates an "ego buffer" against psychological threats.

Furthermore, by transcending the dichotomous limitations of the existing "Active-Passive Use Model," this study proves that the direction of well-being is determined by the order and sequence of activities. This provides a sophisticated direction for the future of SNS research. Moving beyond the negative perspective that views social media merely as points of addiction or comparison, I believe the most significant contribution of this study is that it reveals the potential of SNS as a tool for public health policy and depression care through the strategic arrangement of activities.

## Summary

- This paper shows that self-affirmation activities on Instagram can function as an ego buffer against the negative emotions caused by upward social comparison.
- The study expands SNS well-being research by emphasizing that effects differ not only by activity type but also by activity sequence.
- The findings open discussion on long-term well-being impact, alternative behavioral combinations, and ethical platform-level intervention.
